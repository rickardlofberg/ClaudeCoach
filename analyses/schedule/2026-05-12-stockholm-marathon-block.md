## Phase 1 — Marathon taper (May 12–29)

### Taper philosophy

The work is done. The 32 km long run happened. Taper means: don't dig a new hole, keep the legs moving and sharp, arrive at the start line fresh and slightly itchy to run. The biggest risk in a taper is doing too much, not too little.

**Volume target per week:** ~40 km this week, ~33 km next week, ~20 km race week (pre-race).

## Stockholm Marathon race plan — May 30

### Target and pacing

**Goal:** 3:38–3:43 (4:59–5:19/km overall; start 5:15–5:20/km). Negative split if possible.

Your 1:40:20 HM fitness projects a 3:29–3:33 all-out marathon. We're targeting 3:38–3:43 intentionally:
- DXT is 13 days later — don't leave everything in Stockholm.
- Marathon-specific training (marathon-pace tempo runs, higher weekly volume) hasn't been the focus of the last 4 weeks.
- A controlled race with a strong finish > a brave race with a crawl to the line.

**Pacing bands:**
- **Km 1–10:** 5:18–5:22/km. Hold the crowd back. The race feels easy here; that's correct. HR should sit around 155–160.
- **Km 10–30:** 5:12–5:18/km. Settle into race pace. HR 158–165. If you're feeling genuinely good by km 20, you can push toward the lower end.
- **Km 30–42.2:** Stay disciplined. This is where marathons are lost. If you've run the first 30 smart, the last 12 is a matter of maintaining. HR will drift to 165–172 naturally — that's fine, that's racing. Don't chase a PR feeling at km 35.

### Fuelling

- **Before:** Normal breakfast 2–3 h out, familiar food.
- **Gel every 8–10 km** from km 8 onwards: km 8, 16, 24, 32 (optionally 38). 4–5 gels total. Marathon is not an endurance-nutrition event like the 100 km, but 40+ gels is better than bonking at km 35.
- **Water at every station.** Don't wait until you're thirsty.

### Knee management

- Walk the aid stations. This isn't a concession — it's nutrition strategy and a 20-second relief valve for the knee.
- If the knee starts to talk to you before km 30, ease up. Stockholm is B-priority; Sörmlands is A. **DNF over injury that costs you September.**

### Weather / conditions

Check the forecast the morning of. If it's warm (>16°C), target the conservative end of the pace range and add a water station.

## Phase 2 — Post-marathon recovery + DXT (May 31 – Jun 12)

### Reality check on the window

Marathon day is May 30. DXT is June 12. That is 13 days. Normal marathon recovery guideline is ~1 easy day per race km = 42 days before resuming hard training. In 13 days, you will not be recovered. That's fine — DXT is a fun mountain run, not a race with a time target.

**Goal for this block:** Keep the legs mobile, get blood flowing, arrive at DXT able to hike and run comfortably for 22 km in the mountains. Nothing more.

### DXT race strategy

**Framing:** You're 13 days post-marathon running a mountain trail race in the Dolomites. The legs will not be at full strength. That's known going in. This is a beautiful day in the mountains, not a performance test.

- **Hike all uphills.** This is not a concession — top ultra runners hike steep climbs because it's faster and cheaper on the legs than running them. Get good at hiking fast (poles if allowed/brought).
- **Run comfortable on the flats and descents** — the descents in the Dolomites will be technical and quad-burning. Save your legs here; this is where marathon legs complain.
- **No time target.** Finish, enjoy the scenery, eat the food at aid stations.
- **DNF over injury** — Sörmlands 100 is Sep 12. A twisted ankle or tendon flare here costs 4–6 weeks of the ultra build.
- **Gear:** Check the course profile before packing. Poles may be useful; a mandatory gear list likely applies for a Dolomites race.

## What I'm watching for

- **Post-marathon morning HR (Jun 1–4).** Tells you how hard the marathon hit. High and slow to drop = take Jun 5–6 easier than written above.
- **Knee on the first run back (Jun 3).** Marathon distance loads the patella and ITB significantly. If it's louder than pre-race, shorten Jun 5–6 and skip DXT intensity (which is already low).
- **DXT downhills.** Quads will be the limiting factor at 13 days post-marathon on technical descent. Go slower on the down than you think you need to.
- **How Jun 6 feels.** The 12–14 km run on Jun 6 (6 days post-marathon) is the canary. If it feels labored and HR is high, pull back the Jun 9 Kundo and extend recovery into Jun 13+.
