# Race plan — Sörmlands 100, Sat Sep 12 2026

Context: `analyses/season-plan/2026-05-12-season-plan.md` (A-race, finish goal, first ultra) and `analyses/schedule/2026-08-24-weekly-review.md` (current taper, running through this race). Course details from sormlands100.com.

**Read in one line: this is a finish-goal race with generous cutoffs once past the first 23 km — the main risks are fueling and fatigue management over a much longer effort than anything in training, not raw pace.**

## Course facts

- **Distance:** 102.8 km point-to-point (branded "100 km"). **+1300 m** vert. Terrain: 60% trail, 30% gravel, 10% asphalt.
- **Start:** 00:00 (midnight) Sat Sep 12, Öster Malma, Nyköping. **Finish:** Nävekvarns Folkets Park.
- Midnight start means a substantial stretch of the race — at minimum the first several hours, likely into the Nyköping area depending on pace — is run in full darkness. Headlamp + backup power is mandatory gear, not optional.

## Cutoffs — and where the real pressure is

| Checkpoint | km | Cutoff (elapsed) | Cumulative pace required |
|---|---|---|---|
| Likstammen | 11.0 | 03:00 | 16:22/km |
| Tystberga | 23.2 | 05:00 | **12:56/km** |
| Nyköping | 56.9 | 14:00 | 14:46/km |
| Nävsjön | 92.9 | 21:00 | 13:34/km |
| Finish (Dammkärr/Nävekvarn) | 102.8 | 24:00 | 14:01/km |

**The tightest margin in the whole race is the first 23 km** (Tystberga's 12:56/km cumulative, ~4.6 km/h including stops) — tighter than the overall finish requirement. Every checkpoint after that has more slack, not less. Practical implication: don't over-linger at the first two aid stations, and don't let night-time technical-terrain caution eat too much time before Tystberga. Once through there, the schedule relaxes substantially.

Aid stations: 9 total, at 11.0 / 23.2 / 33.1 / 45.7 / 56.9 / 71.8 / 84.4 / 92.9 / 102.8 km — max ~13 km apart. Most offer warm food, fruit, candy, Umara, and water.

## Pacing target: ~15-18 hours, effort-capped not pace-chasing

Goal is finishing, not time — the season plan has always framed this as a finish goal. Given that, the target below is a planning anchor, not something to chase if the day goes differently.

**Why 15-18h:** extrapolating from Ultravasan 45 (Aug 15) — 44 km at a controlled avg HR 148, no cardiovascular fade even as pace faded in the back half — scaled up for 2.3x the distance, more technical terrain, the midnight start, and the normal ultra positive-split, this range keeps 6-9 hours of cutoff buffer. That's the right amount of margin for a first 100 km, especially coming off the recent heavy training block.

**Strategy: HR-capped effort, same philosophy that's worked all season.**
- Through the night and first half: stay mostly in the **easy zone (<145)**. This is the same discipline that made Ultravasan 45 controlled rather than a grind.
- Second half: effort will naturally drift into **steady (145-155)** as fatigue suppresses HR at the same pace — that's expected, not a red flag, and matches the pattern already seen in DXT and Ultravasan 45. Don't chase a specific HR number late; chase feel.
- Never chase pace or splits directly. Splits are a sanity check, not a target.

**Reference splits** (illustrative only — assumes a flat 16h pace of 9:20/km cumulative; reality will run faster early, slower late):

| Checkpoint | km | Reference arrival (16h pace) | Cutoff | Buffer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Likstammen | 11.0 | ~1:43 | 3:00 | 1:17 |
| Tystberga | 23.2 | ~3:37 | 5:00 | 1:23 |
| Nyköping | 56.9 | ~8:52 | 14:00 | 5:08 |
| Nävsjön | 92.9 | ~14:28 | 21:00 | 6:32 |
| Finish | 102.8 | ~16:00 | 24:00 | 8:00 |

If meaningfully behind this reference by Nyköping (56.9 km, roughly halfway), that's the point to consciously reassess against the still-generous 14h/21h cutoffs rather than panic — there's real room there.

## Fueling

- **Baseline: ~70-80 kcal/h from Maurten Gel 100 (100 kcal each) + Umara sportdrink** — roughly one gel every 75-80 min as the carried floor.
- **Plus real food at aid stations** (warm food, fruit, candy available at all 9) **and a drop bag.** This baseline-plus-real-food combination is a sound structure — the low carried rate isn't a risk on its own once aid-station eating is accounted for, since stations are never more than ~13 km / roughly 1.5-2h apart.
- **Open item:** which aid station(s) allow drop bags isn't confirmed from the course page. Nyköping (56.9 km, roughly the halfway point, one of the more generous cutoffs) is the logical single-drop-bag choice if only one is allowed — worth confirming with the organizer before race day.
- Hydrate at every station regardless of thirst level given the midnight start and however warm Sep 12 turns out to be; the mandatory-gear minimum is 1L bottle/bladder capacity.

## Support

No pacers (not part of this race's format). Some friends may provide informal support at aid stations — not yet confirmed which ones. Worth locking down before race week so it can factor into the plan (a known friendly face at, say, Nyköping or Nävsjön is worth planning around).

## What I'm watching for between now and race day

- **The right hip / general soreness from the Aug 15-23 load cluster.** The current taper (`schedule/2026-08-24-weekly-review.md`) exists specifically to let this resolve — nothing here changes that plan.
- **Night-running unfamiliarity.** Nothing in this season's training has been an explicit night run. If there's a low-cost opportunity during the taper's easy runs (e.g. one short evening/dark shakeout with the actual race headlamp) to test the gear and get comfortable with darkness, it's worth it — not worth adding real training stress for.
- **Drop bag station and friend-support locations** — both open items above, resolve before race week.
